How TicketScout compares ticket prices

Every event page checks up to 12 sellers live and shows you the lowest genuine price first. Here's exactly how, and who we actually check.

What "compare" actually means here

When you open an event page, TicketScout queries each seller's own live catalogue — Ticketmaster's official inventory alongside resale marketplaces — at the moment you load the page, not from a cached snapshot from days ago. Prices from different currencies are converted to GBP using live exchange rates so every row is a fair, like-for-like comparison. The cheapest genuine price is highlighted; listings that look implausibly low compared to everything else for the same event (a common sign of a stale or broken listing, not a real deal) are filtered out rather than shown as a false best price.

We link you directly to the seller to complete your purchase. TicketScout doesn't sell tickets, doesn't hold your payment details, and doesn't add any booking fee of our own on top of what the seller charges.

What we don't do

  • We don't add a booking fee. The price you see (where a seller provides one) is theirs, not ours plus a markup.
  • We don't accept payment to rank a seller higher, or to award the Trusted Seller badge.
  • We don't sell tickets ourselves or hold your money — every purchase happens directly on the seller's own site.

A note on resale prices

Several of the sellers we check are resale marketplaces, where prices are set by individual sellers and can be above or below the ticket's original face value depending on demand. We show what's actually listed, but always confirm the final price — including any fees the seller itself charges — on their site before completing a purchase.
